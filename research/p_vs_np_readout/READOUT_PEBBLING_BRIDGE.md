# Readout–Pebbling Bridge: Retention, Forgetting, and Recomputation

**Status:** model reconciliation + exact finite diagnostics.  
**Claim boundary:** pebbling lower bounds apply to the declared DAG-evaluation model; they do **not** by
themselves lower-bound arbitrary SAT algorithms or prove `P != NP`.

The fixed-order future-readout theorem gives a static retained-width lower bound.  A general machine can
avoid such a bound by forgetting an intermediate value and recomputing it later.  IDM therefore needs a
resource object that prices both retention and reacquisition.

Pebble games are the natural exact model for this step.

---

## 1. DAG evaluator as a retained-state process

Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite directed acyclic computation graph.  Every source value is directly readable;
every non-source value `v` is generated by a declared local rule from its immediate predecessors
`pred(v)`.

A retained evaluator state at step `t` is

\[
R_t\subseteq V,
\]

where `v in R_t` means the exact value at node `v` is currently retained and may be used without
recomputation.

Admissible operations are:

1. retain a source value;
2. retain `v` when every node in `pred(v)` is currently retained;
3. forget any retained node;
4. repeat earlier computation in order to reacquire a forgotten node.

The terminal query is the sink value.

---

## 2. Exact black-pebbling correspondence

Place a black pebble on `v` iff `v in R_t`.  Then the retained-evaluator rules are exactly the black
pebble rules:

- sources may be pebbled directly;
- a non-source may be pebbled only when all predecessors are pebbled;
- a pebble may be removed at any time;
- a forgotten value can return only by replaying a legal dependency chain.

Therefore every execution in this declared evaluator model induces a black pebbling of `G`, and every
black pebbling induces a legal retained-value schedule.

Under this translation,

\[
\boxed{
S=\max_t |R_t|
}

is retained space and the number of placement/recomputation operations is a finite work/time ledger.
Any black-pebbling space or time-space lower bound transfers immediately to this evaluator model.

This is the recomputation-aware extension of the Readout Genesis rule: a distinction may be discarded
only if the system either no longer needs it or pays the causal work needed to reconstruct it.

---

## 3. Exact finite diagnostic

`pebbling_readout_frontier.py` performs exhaustive breadth-first search over pebble configurations for
small pyramid DAGs.  For each declared space budget `S` it finds the shortest legal pebbling that
reaches the sink or certifies that no such schedule exists within that finite state graph.

For the pyramid family with height `h` in the script's convention, the first feasible space budgets on
small instances are:

| h | nodes | first feasible S |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 3 | 3 |
| 2 | 6 | 4 |
| 3 | 10 | 5 |
| 4 | 15 | 6 |

The script also reports the minimum number of moves for each tested space budget, exposing the
retention/recomputation trade-off rather than hiding it behind a single runtime number.

These are exact finite diagnostics, not an asymptotic pebbling theorem.

---

## 4. Connection to proof complexity

Pebbling contradictions translate DAG dependency/recomputation structure into unsatisfiable CNFs.
Resolution proof complexity has a substantial literature proving proof-length/proof-space trade-offs on
such formulas.  In the SAT-solver reading, proof length corresponds roughly to work and proof space to
retained clauses/information for resolution-style reasoning.

This gives the P-vs-NP research lane a controlled second rung:

\[
\text{retained DAG evaluation}
\to
\text{pebbling}
\to
\text{resolution / restricted proof systems}.
\]

The rung is mathematically useful because recomputation is explicitly priced, unlike the fixed-order
OBDD model.

---

## 5. The semantic-shortcut obstruction

A general SAT decider is not obliged to evaluate a prescribed DAG, preserve a particular set of
clauses, or generate a resolution refutation.  It may exploit an equivalent representation or a global
algebraic property that bypasses the dependency schedule.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{hard pebbling DAG/CNF}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{hard for every polynomial-time SAT algorithm}.
}
\]

This is the same simulation-bridge problem encountered in the OBDD lane, now with recomputation made
explicit.

Any attempt to promote a pebbling lower bound to `P != NP` must first prove that the proposed
translation is unavoidable for **all** deterministic polynomial-time algorithms.  Assuming such an
unavoidable simulation would merely hide the desired lower bound in the model definition.

---

## 6. Cost ledger for the next attacks

Every future candidate should report at least

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C=(T,S,Q,R,W)
}

where

- `T` = transition/work count;
- `S` = maximum retained mutable state in bits/words;
- `Q` = input/query probes;
- `R` = recomputation/re-read count;
- `W` = maximum live boundary width or proof object width where applicable.

A theorem about only one coordinate must not silently be promoted to a lower bound on the whole
computation.

---

## 7. Current frontier

The branch now contains three increasingly general obstruction patterns:

1. **future-readout width:** exact exponential width under fixed ordered read-once access;
2. **pebbling:** retention versus recomputation on an explicit causal DAG;
3. **weak rank principle:** algebraic invariant obstruction with current proof-complexity hardness.

The missing step common to all three is a **semantic-shortcut-resistant bridge** to unrestricted
polynomial-time computation.  That bridge—not another finite collision—is the load-bearing P-vs-NP
research problem.
