# Compressed Negative Closure: Sound Core and Route Audit

**Status:** exact finite soundness + executable DAG checker; unrestricted polynomial-size bridge OPEN.

## 1. Why the negative side is the real bottleneck

For SAT under restriction,

\[
SAT(F)=SAT(F|_{x_i=0})\vee SAT(F|_{x_i=1}).
\]

A positive candidate claim can be audited along one child path: either a local recursion defect appears, or a true terminal leaf yields an actual satisfying assignment.  A negative claim is different:

\[
SAT(F)=0
\iff
SAT(F|_{x_i=0})=0\ \wedge\ SAT(F|_{x_i=1})=0.
\]

Hence a negative claim needs a closure object that accounts for both children, unless some stronger local rule certifies many branches at once.

## 2. Finite negative-closure certificate

Let `State` be a finite restriction state and `Choice` a declared split.  Assume the exact split law

\[
sat(s)=sat(L(s,c))\vee sat(R(s,c)).
\]

Let `leaf_reject(s)=1` be an independently checkable local contradiction with the soundness condition

\[
leaf\_reject(s)=1\Longrightarrow sat(s)=0.
\]

A negative certificate is generated by

1. a sound rejected leaf; or
2. a split whose left and right children both carry negative certificates.

`formal/IDM_CompressedNegativeClosure.v` proves

\[
\boxed{NegCert(s)\Longrightarrow sat(s)=0.}
\]

The proof is purely finite and uses only the split law plus leaf soundness.

## 3. Sharing is allowed

The executable certificate is a DAG, not an unfolded tree.  Identical normalized restriction states may be stored once and referenced multiple times.  The checker derives each child state from the parent formula and split variable, so stored references cannot manufacture a different branch.

`compressed_negative_closure.py` includes an exact two-variable UNSAT fixture where both children of the root normalize to the same restricted formula and both grandchildren normalize to the same contradiction.  The certificate therefore uses

\[
3\text{ unique DAG nodes}
\]

instead of

\[
7\text{ nodes in the unfolded binary tree}.
\]

A deliberately corrupted leaf is rejected by the independent verifier.

## 4. Readout / EPSC-style quantitative placement

The restriction-defect kernel already gives a partial-tree inequality

\[
d_{root}\le
\sum_{v\in observed}\Delta_v+
\sum_{\ell\in frontier}\beta_\ell.
\]

A closed negative-closure DAG is the zero-tail case: every frontier obligation is replaced by a sound local contradiction or by two recursively discharged children.  If a frontier remains uncertified, the correct Readout state is `HOLD`, not an assumed zero remainder.

Thus the imported quantitative architecture is used directly:

\[
\text{local certified defects} + \text{certified tail}
\Longrightarrow
\text{global output control}.
\]

## 5. Critical route closure

The soundness theorem is not the missing P-vs-NP lower bound.  The hard statement would be one of the following.

### Route A: negative-closure lower bound

Exhibit an explicit UNSAT family `F_n` such that every valid closure DAG has superpolynomial size.

This is a lower bound for a concrete branching/closure proof system.  By itself it does **not** rule out an unrestricted SAT circuit, because an arbitrary circuit is not required to emit this certificate system.

### Route B: circuit-to-closure compression bridge

Prove that every size-`s` correct SAT circuit `C` and every negative input `F` induce a valid negative-closure DAG of size

\[
poly(s,|F|).
\]

Then a superpolynomial closure lower bound would imply a circuit lower bound.

This bridge is **not** currently proved.  The naive audit does not give it: even with a small reusable candidate circuit, recursively validating a negative claim may visit exponentially many distinct restriction states.

## 6. Why this is a genuine new premise, not bookkeeping

A polynomial-size, polynomial-time-checkable negative certificate for every UNSAT formula would make UNSAT admit short NP-style certificates.  Therefore a universal polynomial closure theorem is a major proof-complexity statement, not an automatic consequence of Readout Genesis, RRR, or circuit sharing.

Accordingly, the P-vs-NP lane must not claim

\[
small\ circuit\Rightarrow small\ negative\ closure
\]

without an explicit theorem that preserves unrestricted circuit power.

## 7. Current load-bearing target

The strongest faithful target is now candidate-adaptive and lineage-aware:

> Given a small candidate circuit `C` making a negative claim on `F`, use only the circuit DAG, local restriction evaluations, and sound syntax-level closure rules to either
>
> 1. produce a concrete local/leaf defect, or
> 2. produce a compressed negative certificate whose size is bounded in terms of the actual reusable lineage of `C`.

The missing theorem is a nontrivial **lineage-compression bound**.  If no such polynomial bound exists, the alternative is to exploit the failure itself to construct a refuting restriction/input.

## 8. Claim boundary

Proved / executable in this lane:

- SAT restriction self-reduction is the declared local law;
- positive candidate claims admit witness-or-defect audit;
- negative closure certificates are sound;
- identical restricted states can be shared in a DAG;
- corrupted certificates are rejected;
- uncertified frontier obligations remain `HOLD`.

Still OPEN:

- polynomial-size negative closure for arbitrary negative SAT instances;
- polynomial circuit-to-negative-closure compression;
- superpolynomial lower bound for the unrestricted SAT target;
- `SAT notin P/poly` and `P != NP`.
